The proposal in brief

Four generations, one smokehouse, a story worth telling properly

The smokehouses above Craster harbour were built in 1856. James William Robson came to the village in 1890, and four generations later the Robson family still turns herring into kippers by the same method: split, brined, hung on tenterhooks and smoked for up to 16 hours over whitewood shavings and oak sawdust.

Craster once had four smokehouses. Today L. Robson & Sons is the only one left, and its kippers have a reputation as some of the finest in Britain. That history, craft and sense of place is exactly what a well-made film can carry to new customers, trade buyers and visitors.

This proposal has two parts: a documentary that tells the Craster Kippers story in full, and a stock library of footage and photographs for the website, shop, social media, trade and press, ready to use whenever you need it.

Part 1 · The film

The history, the process and the values

A documentary in three movements, told by the Robson family and the people around them, and shot over a full smoking cycle so the viewer sees, hears and almost smells the work.

History

The 1856 smokehouses, the herring fleets and the herring girls, and James William Robson’s arrival in 1890. Family photographs and memories bring more than a century of Robson kippers to life, with illustration and animation where the archive runs out.

Process

A day in the smokehouse, from first light. Splitting, the brine bath, hanging the fish on tenterhooks, lighting the shavings and sawdust, and the long, slow smoke. Then the shop, the packing room and the postal crates heading out across the country.

Values

Tradition, family and place. Why the method has barely changed since 1900, why quality comes before quantity, and why, as Neil Robson puts it, you just can’t make Craster Kippers anywhere else.

Made to be used, not shelved. The full film works on your website, on a screen in the shop and at food festivals and trade events. The trailer and social cut-downs bring people to it, and to the online store.

Every interview is planned with the family first, so the film sounds like you, and nothing is published until you are happy with it.

Part 2 · The stock library

Photographs and footage you can use for years

A library of your own images means no more scrambling for a photo when a magazine, a stockist or a new product launch needs one. Part is shot alongside the film, and part on a second day a few weeks later, for different light, weather and activity.

The smokehouse

The building, the smoke, the hooks and racks, the fires being lit and the light coming through the haze.

The people

Portraits of the family and team at work, and the hands-on detail of splitting, hanging and packing.

The product

Kippers, smoked salmon and fillets on the bench, in the shop, packed for post and served on the plate.

The place

Craster harbour, the village, the coast and Dunstanburgh, in different light and weather.

Ready to use

Edited, colour-graded and delivered in folders by theme, with captions and keywords so anyone can find the right image quickly.

Yours to keep

Licensed for unlimited use in L. Robson & Sons’ own marketing, with no time limit and no further fees.
