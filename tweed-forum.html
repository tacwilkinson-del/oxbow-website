A proposal for Tweed Forum

Keeping the Tweed in the spotlight

A 12-month communications retainer to make Tweed Forum’s work visible to the public, policy makers, partners and funders, and to reinforce its place as one of the leading river organisations in Scotland and northern England.

Prepared by Tommy Wilkinson, OxbowProgramme year: January to December 2027Confidential
The proposal in brief

Leading work deserves to be seen as leading

Tweed Forum delivers some of the most ambitious river and catchment restoration in the UK, from the Breamish at Harehope to the Eddleston Water. The people who fund and deliver this work know how good it is. This programme makes sure everyone else does too.

The aim is simple: to reinforce Tweed Forum’s position as one of the leading river organisations in Scotland and northern England, and to keep that position visible all year, not only when a big project completes.

Over 12 months, Oxbow will deliver a joined-up mix of short social films, two flagship documentaries and a monthly podcast. Each piece links to the next, each is signed off by Tweed Forum, and every quarter you get a report on spend and results against agreed KPIs.

Four audiences, one story

The public

See a river charity that gets results on the ground, and feel part of the change.

Policy makers

See a trusted delivery partner whose evidence can shape catchment policy.

Partners

Farmers, landowners, agencies and charities see that working with Tweed Forum pays off, and is recognised.

Funders

See a stable, well-run programme that turns investment into measurable improvement.

Why now, and why a retainer

The audience is already there. Now it needs a reason to stay.

Two Tweed Forum films made with Oxbow have shown what happens when the work is told well. Together they have passed 237,000 YouTube views.

One film makes a spike. A programme builds an audience. After each release the views climbed, then the channel went quiet until the next project. Viewers, journalists and the YouTube algorithm all reward a steady rhythm. Without it, the attention those films earned starts to fade.

The timing is right. The Breamish restoration is complete, the Till programme has a decade of results to show, and funders are looking harder than ever for proof that nature investment delivers. A planned year of content turns that track record into lasting profile.

  • One partner who knows the storyNo time lost on briefing. I already know the catchment, the people and the projects.
  • Planned around the seasonsUpland filming, river levels and launches are booked ahead, not squeezed in.
  • Lower cost per pieceThe retainer rate is 10% below the cost of commissioning each piece on its own.
  • Faster sign-offGuardrails and approval steps are agreed once, at the start, and used all year.
Lawton, applied to communications

More, bigger, better and joined up

Sir John Lawton’s 2010 review, Making Space for Nature, called for wildlife sites that are more, bigger, better and joined up. Tweed Forum lives that idea across the catchment. This programme applies the same four principles to how the work is told.

More

More touchpoints. Something new every month, instead of one film every year or two.

Bigger

Bigger stories for bigger audiences. Two flagship films about landscape-scale change.

Better

Better quality and better evidence. Data-led updates, a consistent brand and results you can measure.

Joined up

Every piece links to the next. Shorts lead to films, films lead to the podcast, and partners share each other’s stories.

Strand 2 · Film 1

Headwaters: restoring the upland source of the River Breamish

This film presents the River Till restoration as what it has become: an evolving, stable and investible programme. It has moved from Harehope to Hedgeley and from Ingram to Linhope, with each project building on the last. The story is catchment thinking and long-term improvement.

The uplands are the hook. Wild headwaters capture the imagination of the public and investors alike, and they let us show that farmers and landowners are working hard to help our watercourses, right at the source.

4 days scoping and sign-off5 days filming10 days editing1 day contingencyLaunch: September 2027
Strand 3 · Film 2

Northumberland’s rivers: Tweed Forum and the Tyne Rivers Trust

A region-wide positioning film that features Tweed Forum alongside the Tyne Rivers Trust. The two organisations do not work together, and their projects are different. Seen side by side, their work shows Northumberland as a place where significant river restoration is happening, and happening at pace.

The Tweed and the Tyne are among the finest rivers in the UK, known for their salmon and their landscapes. The film shows that, although the projects differ, the region’s approach is joined up and collaborative, and that Northumberland is the place to watch, and to invest in, for river restoration.

This film depends on the Tyne Rivers Trust agreeing to take part. As a regional story, it is well suited to partner funding: see how partner funding could work.

Why this works

More output, less risk, and less pressure on your team

A retainer is more than a cheaper way to buy films. It changes how communications fit around Tweed Forum’s work.

Less pressure on your staff

Planning, filming, editing, publishing and reporting are handled for you. Your team gives input and signs off, then gets back to delivering projects.

Lower risk

One contract, a fixed price for the year and contingency built into each film. Quarterly reviews let us adjust the plan before anything goes off track.

A trusted, well-connected partner

I already know Tweed Forum’s projects, staff and landowners, and work with the agencies, estates and charities around them. That trust opens doors and gets people talking on camera.

New touchpoints, new audiences

Short films, documentaries, screenings and a podcast reach people in different places and at different moments, including many who have not heard of Tweed Forum yet.

A library that keeps working

Every shoot produces stills, clips and interviews that Tweed Forum can reuse in funding bids, reports, talks, events and partner channels for years.

Evidence for every bid

Quarterly reports turn views and engagement into clear numbers that support funding applications, board papers and conversations with policy makers.

How partner funding could work

Shared stories, shared costs

Several parts of this programme serve goals that reach beyond Tweed Forum. Sharing those costs with the organisations that benefit could cut what Tweed Forum pays by more than 40%, while every partner gets content it can use.

Tweed Forum

Holds the contract and leads the programme. Funds the short films and reporting, most of the podcast, half of Headwaters and a share of the Northumberland film.

Tyne Rivers Trust

Co-star of the Northumberland film, and the only part of the programme it is asked to fund. The film puts its work in front of new regional audiences.

Environment Agency

A partner in the Breamish restoration at Harehope. Both films show its investment in river restoration delivering results, and its £4,000 towards the podcast brings its work to a new listening audience.

Natural England

Also a partner at Harehope. Both films show Lawton’s principles of nature recovery working at catchment scale.

NEMSA

The North East Mayoral Strategic Authority is backing nature recovery and investment across the region. The Northumberland film positions the region as a place where river restoration is happening at pace.
